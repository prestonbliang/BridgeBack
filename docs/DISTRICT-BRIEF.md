# BridgeBack: a practical San Diego demonstration

Prepared for a Congressional App Challenge presentation to Representative Scott Peters and district reviewers. This brief emphasizes the local public-service problem and demonstrable engineering. It does not claim his endorsement, an office partnership or knowledge of his personal preferences. His current issue and challenge pages could not be read from this environment, so no current policy position or local judging rule is attributed to him.

## Opening: the problem a person can recognize

“A person can find a housing program and still get stuck at the paperwork. If they cannot get a pay stub, another list of links does not answer the next question: what can they do now? BridgeBack shows what the missing paper is holding up, checks listed alternatives, and helps them take a focused question to a real organization.”

## The five details to show

1. **Useful before a profile.** Overview → Find a real person to help. The current saved official search lists ten counselors; four match rental counseling within 25 miles of the 92101 search point. The date, distance basis and limited coverage are visible. It is not a claim of appointments, full county coverage or eligibility.
2. **Original code with an observable result.** Try the fictional demo. Preview a missing pay stub, then explicitly apply it. In the untouched demo, two forms become available and readiness changes from 40% to 60%. Those are calculated fixture results, not promised outcomes for residents.
3. **An answer when the first route fails.** Mark the pay stub unavailable and compare each plan’s accepted alternatives. Show an impossible required-document route too. The app must admit when its listed options cannot solve the problem.
4. **Help that continues after an explanation.** Open step support, prepare questions, choose a real public contact, save “waiting” and a personal follow-up date. Contacting someone never counts as possessing a paper. A mistaken completion can be corrected.
5. **Respect for the person’s time and privacy.** No account, uploads, Social Security numbers, advertising or opaque AI ranking. Calculations are explainable and run locally. The iOS candidate bundles its core functions for first-launch offline use and shares only when the person opens the system sheet.

## A candid answer about impact

“The software is tested against specific expected results. We have not measured housing placements or applicant success rates. The next evidence would come from consent-based usability sessions with applicants and helpers: can a person identify the blocker, choose an accepted route, and prepare the right question without mistaking preparation for approval?”

The [22-case deterministic benchmark](evaluation-results.json) and [validation record](VALIDATION.md) support correctness claims. Do not turn the fixture’s 20-percentage-point readiness change into an impact statistic. Do not claim a representative approved the app or that a provider accepted its example rules.

## An answer about government integration

“The real counselor/contact information and educational references come from identified public sources. Application-rule examples are fictional and clearly separated. Publishing real program screening would require reviewed current program-specific rules, service areas, application instructions and update ownership. BridgeBack does not send applications or private profiles to government systems.”

San Diego County is the starting service area. Do not equate county boundaries or a downtown ZIP search with a congressional district boundary. A district office is not presented as a housing placement agency or a route around program requirements.
