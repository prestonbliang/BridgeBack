# Two-minute interactive competition demonstration

Start on Overview → **Explore Demo** or **Try a guided example**. The banner identifies a temporary fictional workspace; personal progress is preserved. Use the seven scene buttons to move between the relevant views. All numerical outcomes are computed from the current state. If you change scenarios, outcomes may differ.

**0:00–0:15 · Scene 1: The problem**
“This fictional applicant has identity and residence information, but income paperwork is holding up two preparation plans. BridgeBack helps answer a specific question: what can I do next that actually changes my situation?” Show the calculated readiness and next action.

**0:15–0:30 · Scene 2: PathFinder**
Open scene 2 in My checklist, then Journey map. Point out the shared pay-stub node and the separate program income gates. “Each program keeps its own rules. Connected requirements reveal why a form is blocked.” Pan or focus one plan, then show the accessible checklist.

**0:30–0:45 · Scene 3: BarrierBreak**
Show the highest-impact barrier and open Why this? “The engine distinguishes downstream consequences from tasks that become available immediately. It shows facts, missing information, assumptions and the fictional source.”

**0:45–1:00 · Scene 4: What-if**
Choose Pay stub in “Choose a step to preview.” Read the actual/hypothetical comparison. In the untouched default demo, that accepted shared route unlocks both forms and advances two income milestones. “This is a counterfactual evaluation using the same engine. The real checklist has not changed.”

**1:00–1:15 · Scene 5: Actual progress**
Choose Update my checklist. Show the new available tasks, updated readiness/recommendation and Activity event. Correct the availability event to demonstrate that dependent tasks block again. No result is scripted into the scene.

**1:15–1:40 · Scene 6: Multi-program optimization and Route Rescue**
Show Documents. A pay stub is explicitly accepted by Fresh Start and Harbor; an employer letter is not accepted by Harbor. Mark pay stub unavailable to show a genuine alternative route. With three selected plans and identity/residence available, the minimum income route needs employer letter plus benefits statement. Mark a required identity document unavailable to demonstrate an honest “no accepted route” result instead of an invented workaround.

**1:40–2:00 · Scene 7: Action plan**
Export the preparation packet. It contains program-specific checklists, accepted alternatives, current status and the next action. Show the privacy explanation and Return to my workspace. “BridgeBack turns a confusing list of obstacles into an explainable path. It doesn’t submit an application or guarantee housing.”

## Additional demonstrations

- “Eligibility changes” switches to fictional household settings. Change income and inspect each program’s distinct explanation. Unknown income produces uncertainty.
- In the personal workspace, choose Help me get started, skip optional questions, close/reopen it and resume. Outside-San-Diego location bypasses irrelevant local screening questions.
- After the offline-ready status appears, disconnect the browser network and reload. Graph calculations, simulations, document updates and packet export remain local. Actual phone installation is a separate device test.

Never claim measured housing outcomes, time savings, partnerships, endorsements or verified real program requirements. The deterministic benchmark measures correctness of fictional fixtures only.

The overview also offers a smaller worked example: choose “See what a pay stub changes.” It calculates the same two form unlocks without creating or editing a saved checklist. Use Help for plain-language definitions of waiting steps, alternatives and checklist progress.

## Support-focused demonstration

From My checklist, choose “Get help with Pay stub,” then “I can’t get the document.” Show the four small help steps and the two programs’ different accepted alternatives. Explain that reviewing help does not mark a paper available.

Open Learn the paperwork, identify gross pay in the fictional exercise, then compare $2,000 every two weeks with $2,000 twice monthly. The annual estimates differ, and the profile is not changed.

Open Talk to someone. Show the source date and the 92101 search boundary on the real CFPB counselor data. Filter by a listed language, select a public contact and review the question sheet. Its questions omit the made-up program names. Set “waiting” and a personal follow-up date, then return to Overview: an open question is visible but application readiness has not increased.

The person controls website visits, phone calls and exports. The app does not claim a call was made, an appointment is available or an application was submitted.
